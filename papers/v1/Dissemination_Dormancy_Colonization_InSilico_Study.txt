 Dissemination, Dormancy and Organ Colonization in a Stochastic
                     Agent-Based Model
              A series of in-silico experiments across five tumour-type configurations
                        Written by Pip Window, for Caitlyn Connor Randels · November 2026
 Status: computational modeling study. All results describe the behaviour of a simulated system, not living biology. Nothing in
                    this report is a medical claim, a prediction about any person, or guidance of any kind.



Abstract
Background. The spread of cells from a primary tumour to distant organs is often described in the literature
as highly inefficient: most cells that leave are lost, some persist in a dormant state, and few go on to form a
lesion. Because these events are rare, slow and spread across organs, a simulated system is a convenient
place to ask which steps of the cascade most strongly shape the final outcome.
Approach. I ran four connected sets of computational experiments: (1) an exploratory single-run model of
detachment, dormancy and colonization; (2) a longer, 800-cell, 120-month model with parameters chosen to
echo qualitative ranges from the literature; (3) a five-configuration model (prostate-, ER+ breast-, lung-,
melanoma- and colorectal-like organ-preference profiles), including a reproduction, a 5,000-run robustness
analysis and a ±20% sensitivity analysis; and (4) a large ensemble of 1,210,000 simulated 120-month histories
across 484 parameter conditions, in which eight abstract model parameters were varied alone, in
combination and at different onset times.
Results. With default settings, the large ensemble cleared 94.5% of the roughly 240 cells released per
simulated history, left a mean of 10.4 dormant cells at month 120 (at least one in every run), and produced
an overt colony in 21.7% of runs (95% CI 20.1–23.4%), 94.7% of them in the configuration's preferred organ.
Parameters acting after a cell had settled (dormancy stability, outgrowth barrier) suppressed overt colonies
to under 1% of runs but left the dormant pool unchanged (about 10.6 cells). Only four single parameters,
pushed far enough, produced runs with neither colonies nor dormant cells in at least 94% of cases: release
rate ×0.01 (93.8%), global organ permissiveness ×0.2 (95.2%), dormant-cell removal ×100 (94.1%) and an
establishment threshold of 0.875–0.90 (94.0–98.3%). The establishment threshold showed a sharp transition
(0.6% of runs at 0.70 versus 86.3% at 0.85). Effects shrank steadily when perturbations began later (87.4% at
month 1, 69.6% at month 36, 40.4% at month 60, ~0% at month 108). The five-configuration table from the
earlier work was reproduced exactly, but each of its rows matched in no more than 0.9% of other random
seeds.
Conclusion. In this model, outcomes were governed mainly by what happened at the moment a cell tried to
settle in an organ, and by how permissive the organs were, rather than by anything downstream of
dormancy. Suppressing colonies and emptying the dormant pool behaved as two separate problems. These
are properties of the model as built and calibrated; they are hypotheses for discussion, not findings about
biology.
1. Background
Metastatic spread is commonly described as a multi-step cascade: cells leave a primary tumour, travel
through the circulation, arrest in a distant organ, and then either die, persist in a dormant state, or eventually
grow into a detectable lesion. Several qualitative features of this cascade are widely discussed in the general
literature: most circulating cells are lost; disseminated cells can remain dormant for long periods; and
different tumour types tend to favour different organs (for example, bone for prostate and some breast
tumours, liver for colorectal tumours, and lung for several others). I do not cite specific studies here; these
patterns are used only as qualitative targets that the model should be able to reproduce, not as data the
model was fitted to.
The work began from a conceptual case study, "Metastasis as Relational Heat-Trace Migration", which
framed the process as a set of independent cells whose detach and attach events are discrete, weighted
transitions, observed through a continuous map of activity. That case study contained no data. The four
experimental sets below turned the idea into runnable models and then asked progressively sharper
questions:
   Can a simple model produce clearance, dormancy and rare colonization at all? (Studies 1 and 2)
   Do different organ-preference profiles produce the expected organ pattern, and how stable is any single
    run? (Study 3)
   Which steps of the cascade, when changed, most reduce both overt colonies and the dormant pool, and
    does timing matter? (Study 4)



2. Methods (brief)
All four studies use discrete-time, agent-based stochastic simulation with monthly steps (Study 1 uses 200
abstract steps). Each simulated cell carries its own state: attached or circulating, the organ it arrested in, a
dormancy depth, and fitness-related traits. At each step, cells may be released, removed in circulation, arrest
in an organ (weighted by that organ's permissiveness for the configuration), pass or fail an establishment
step, enter or deepen dormancy, attempt to exit dormancy, and either form an overt colony or fail and return
to dormancy. A cumulative event-weight tally (clearance +1, detachment +7, failed colonization +2, successful
colonization +14, and, in later code, exit attempt +4) was kept as a bookkeeping index of activity. In Study 4,
cells could be released continuously over 120 months (about 240 per history), and admission at arrest or
reawakening passed through a scored establishment step: a weighted score of four features (0.30, 0.25, 0.25,
0.20) compared with a threshold of 0.65 by default, which became stricter (up to 0.85) when many cells were
active at once. Five organ-preference profiles were used throughout Studies 3 and 4 (Table 1).
Study 4 varied eight abstract parameters: release rate, circulating removal, removal of dormant cells,
dormancy stability, global organ permissiveness, preferred-organ permissiveness, the establishment
threshold, and an outgrowth barrier. Every condition was run with 500 seeds × 5 configurations = 2,500 runs,
reusing the same random draws across conditions so differences reflect the parameter change. Targets were
fixed before combinations were examined: overt colonies in ≤1% of runs, any dormant cell left in ≤10% of
runs, and "full elimination" (a run ending at month 120 with no overt colony and no dormant cell; cells still
circulating in the last months were not counted). Internal consistency checks ran in every condition: cell-
count conservation, agreement of the event-weight tally with event counts, agreement between three
independent records of each colony, and agreement between whole-system runs and runs split into four
parts (19,360 comparisons, 0 mismatches). These checks confirm the bookkeeping is consistent; they do not
validate the biology.

3. Results

3.1 Study 1: exploratory model
A single run of the exploratory model (cells with random energy, plasticity, immune-evasion and organ-fit
traits; 200 steps) ended with 17 cells remaining, 8 detached, 3 in deep dormancy, and an event-weight tally
of 187 across 59 events. Re-running the script on the box gave the same values. Dormancy entry was closely
tied to detachment events, and whether a cell colonized depended on its local fit rather than on any fixed
"genetic" property: cells with identical starting rules could succeed or fail. This was a proof of concept with
one run, so it supports no quantitative statement.

3.2 Study 2: longer model with literature-shaped parameters
The second model followed 800 cells for 120 months, with high circulating loss and a strict colonization
threshold chosen to echo qualitative ranges described in the literature (frequent dissemination, rare
progression, multi-year dormancy, organ-dependent permissiveness). The run ended with 790 cells remaining
at the primary site, 1 detached cell in deep dormancy, 1 overt colony (bone, month 36), and an event-weight
tally of 67 over 28 events; a re-run reproduced these numbers. The qualitative picture (most released cells
lost, a single colony, a persisting dormant cell) matched the target pattern, but again this is a single run.

A ±20% sensitivity analysis (400 five-configuration sets per setting) showed that circulating clearance was the
most influential setting: at ×0.8, colonization rose to 0.39 of runs and the full pattern fell to 0.04 of sets; at

3.4 Study 4: large-ensemble perturbation experiments
Default behaviour. Across 2,500 default runs, about 240 cells were released per history and 94.5% were
cleared. A mean of 10.4 dormant cells remained at month 120 (about 4.3% of those released; at least one in
100% of runs), overt colonies occurred at 0.255 per run and in 21.7% of runs (95% CI 20.1–23.4%), 94.7% of
colonies were in the preferred organ, and about 2.2 cells were still circulating at the end. Clearance, a
persistent dormant pool and organ preference all emerged from the model; none was imposed as an
outcome. The configurations differed (Table 4): the prostate-like profile, with the most permissive preferred
organ (0.85), had the largest dormant pool and the most colonies.

Combinations. Of 112 two-parameter settings, 9 met both targets, and all 9 included the establishment
threshold at 0.85 (best: threshold 0.85 with dormant-cell removal ×20, 98.9%; threshold 0.85 with global

multiplicative (median ratio 1.0 on the dormant pool). Two pairings did better than expected: global plus

Partition test. When one quarter of the simulated population was cut off from the rest for 41 months, its
results drifted from the whole-system result in 74–85% of runs per configuration (100 runs each); after
reconnection and replay, all 500 runs agreed again. This confirms the model can be computed in parts
without changing its answers, a property of the simulation rather than of biology.



4. Discussion
Across all four studies, the model reproduced the qualitative picture used as its target: heavy loss of released
cells, a small and persistent dormant pool, and rare colonies concentrated in each configuration's preferred
organ. The main new information comes from Study 4, where large ensembles allowed rare outcomes to be
measured with useful precision.
Three observations seem most worth discussing. First, in this model, stopping colonies and emptying the
dormant pool are different problems. Parameters acting after settlement suppressed colonies almost
completely without touching the dormant pool, while full elimination required acting at or before settlement,
or removing dormant cells directly and heavily. Second, the steepest response belonged to the establishment
step, the point at which an arriving cell is admitted or rejected. Its switch-like behaviour between 0.70 and
0.85 follows from how that step was built (a fixed weighted score compared with a threshold), so it is better
read as a statement about thresholded admission in general than as evidence about any real mechanism.
Third, onset mattered: in a system where settled cells persist, anything acting upstream loses most of its
effect once the dormant pool has formed.
Study 3 adds a methodological lesson. A single seeded run can match a target table perfectly and still be
unrepresentative: each row of Table 2 recurred in under 1% of other seeds, while the qualitative pattern
recurred in roughly a quarter to nine-tenths of runs depending on the measure. Results from small models
should therefore be reported as distributions, not single draws. The earlier label-only duplication shows why
every configuration needs its own independent inputs and why outputs should be checked against each other
before interpretation.
Finally, organ preference in this model is partly built in. Permissiveness weights both where a cell arrests and
how well it fits, and the preferred organ also has an easier threshold (0.30 versus 0.42). The near-total
concentration of colonies in preferred organs is therefore partly structural, and should not be read as the
model discovering tropism.



5. Limitations
   This is a simulation, not biology. All rates were set by hand to produce the target pattern of high
    clearance, a small dormant pool and rare preferred-organ colonies. None is a measured value, and effect
    sizes would change under a different calibration.
   The eight parameters are abstract dials in a model. They do not correspond to any specific biological
    process, product or procedure, and the results say nothing about what would happen in a person.
   The establishment threshold is strong because of how the admission step was designed. Its dominance is
    a modeling choice.
   Reconciliation: in the original interactive sketch and in the early scripts, the "reconcile" step was cosmetic
    (it relabelled the state as coherent without computing anything), and the early five-configuration code
    had no reconciliation step at all. A real bookkeeping check was added later; it confirms internal
    consistency only.
   The exit-attempt weight of +4 appears in the code but is not documented in the standardized weight list.
    In 22.1% of random-seed runs in Study 3, at least one exit attempt occurred, so those tallies do not
    follow the four-weight formula. Event-weight totals from Study 4 (about 1,913 per run, dominated by +7
    per released cell) cannot be compared with those of Study 3 (30–71).
   The first multi-configuration runs were identical except for their labels and are excluded from all results
    above. I have the finding from an earlier comparison but not the original files, so their exact numbers are
    not reproduced here.
   Studies 1 and 2 are single runs. Study 3 "clearance" is about 72% of dissemination events, set directly by
    its clearance probability; by population, about 98% of its 400 cells never left the primary site. In Study 3,
    the prostate-like dormant count of 0 depends on the threshold: one cell sat exactly at the 0.5 cutoff.
   Two variables in the Study 3 code (an immune-evasion trait and a per-cell weight) are initialized but
    never used.
   Sets of four to six parameters in Study 4 were tested only at moderate strength, so the comparison
    across set sizes is not like-for-like. Cells still circulating at month 120 (about 2.2 per default run) were not
    counted against full elimination.
   Not modeled: secondary seeding from established colonies, distinct immune compartments, organ-
    specific growth dynamics, or any real imaging or patient data.



6. Conclusion
Over four studies and more than 1.2 million simulated histories, a simple agent-based model of
dissemination, dormancy and colonization reproduced the qualitative pattern it was built to show, and its
large-ensemble experiments gave a consistent internal picture. Outcomes depended most on the
establishment step and on organ permissiveness. Suppressing colonies and clearing the dormant pool
behaved as separate problems. Effects acting upstream faded with later onset. A few strong parameter
changes outperformed many moderate ones. These are statements about this model under its chosen
calibration. They are offered as hypotheses for discussion and further modeling, with no clinical meaning.
